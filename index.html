How the optimiser works

The desk solves the standard day-ahead arbitrage program: choose charge c_t and discharge d_t in each settlement period to maximise trading revenue net of degradation.

max  Σ_t p_t · (d_t − c_t) · Δt − κ · Σ_t d_t · Δt
s.t.  E_min ≤ E_0 + Σ (η·c_t − d_t/η)·Δt ≤ E_max
      0 ≤ c_t, d_t ≤ P̄  ·  E_T ≥ E_0

The program is solved exactly by dynamic programming on a 200-step discretised state of charge. Each period allows charge, discharge or hold, capped by the power rating and cell headroom, so the recovered schedule respects every constraint by construction. With perfect foresight of realised prices this is an upper bound on achievable profit; production desks re-run it rolling-horizon on price forecasts, as described in the references.

References

  1. Optimized Operation of Standalone BESS in Cross-Market Energy Arbitrage · arXiv 2509.21337, MILP formulation and backtest framing.
  2. Designing an energy arbitrage strategy with linear programming · S. Klosterman, LP on NYISO day-ahead data.
  3. Elering Nord Pool price API · Estonian bidding zone, the dataset used on this page.

The naive benchmark charges in the cheapest quartile of periods and discharges in the priciest quartile, ignoring the SOC trajectory. The gap between it and the optimum is what the scheduler is paid for.
